# FCP Epistemic Rules

## 0. Truth-seeking supremacy

The ultimate purpose of FCP is to improve our justified understanding of physical reality. Its methods, comparison keys, subtraction rules, framework taxonomy, and accumulated conclusions are instruments rather than protected commitments.

If evidence or reasoning shows that any FCP method systematically obstructs accurate inference about reality, it should be revised or abandoned through explicit prospective governance rather than defended for continuity's sake. Such revision does not silently rewrite historical results.

Convergence is evidence only to the degree justified by independence, specificity, physical realization, and empirical support. Non-convergence is not evidence that no deeper reality exists. No mathematical or comparative result is promoted to physical truth without the bridges required by the rules below.

## 1. No-framework privilege

All frameworks, including reduced NFC and the null competitor, are evaluated under the same evidentiary standard. A framework's vocabulary may define a question but may not define the answer in its own favor.

## 2. Preserve results, not theories

If a result survives after its original interpretation fails, retain the result under the weakest accurate formulation. Do not preserve terminology merely to preserve lineage.

## 3. Anti-smuggling rule

Every material structure entering an argument must be classified by origin. A proof may not silently import:

- a preferred ontology;
- background spacetime or dimensionality;
- a state space or Hilbert structure;
- a probability measure;
- locality;
- a continuum limit;
- a physical observable map;
- a realization functor;
- a dynamics or history selector;
- empirical constants or calibration values.

If such structure is required, name it as an assumption, model choice, physical bridge, or empirical input.

## 4. Source and evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest available level and never promote upward without an explicit bridge:

1. **Primary authoritative source** — canonical paper, monograph, repository state, standard, or official record actually defining the framework/result.
2. **Source-bound derivation** — a reconstruction whose dependency closure is tied to primary sources.
3. **Independent mathematical verification** — proof, formalization, or replay establishing the mathematical claim at its declared hypotheses.
4. **Countermodel / negative witness** — explicit construction showing that stated premises do not force a stronger conclusion.
5. **Empirical evidence** — observations or experiments with declared provenance, calibration, and comparator.
6. **Secondary interpretation** — review, commentary, historical account, or explanatory synthesis.

Secondary interpretation cannot overwrite primary-source meaning.

## 5. Promotion rule

Every promotion must name its bridge. In particular:

- `mathematical structure -> physical structure` requires a physical-realization bridge;
- `allowed processes -> actual dynamics` requires a law/history selector;
- `quotient-visible -> physically exhaustive` requires an observable-exhaustion bridge;
- `finite stage -> continuum/global statement` requires a limit/coherence theorem;
- `relative canonical -> physically canonical` requires an independent physical-selection theorem;
- `fit to known data -> prediction` requires a genuinely held-out or prospective test.

Absence of a bridge means promotion is forbidden.

## 6. Canonicity levels

Always distinguish:

1. presentation canonicity — invariant under relabeling/representation;
2. relative mathematical canonicity — unique once a domain/category/language is fixed;
3. framework canonicity — selected by the framework's formal rules;
4. physical canonicity — uniquely selected as describing nature;
5. empirical selection — favored by observation over competitors.

Levels 1–3 do not imply levels 4–5.

## 7. Result classifications

Every substantive result must use one primary label:

- `SOURCE_DERIVED`
- `GENERIC_MATHEMATICS`
- `VALID_CONDITIONAL`
- `MODEL_CHOICE`
- `PHYSICAL_BRIDGE`
- `EMPIRICAL`
- `NONFORCED`
- `COUNTERMODELED`
- `OPEN`

Optional scope qualifiers may be added, but must not blur the primary classification.

## 8. Strong-claim test

A framework-specific result receives strong credit only if it:

1. follows from antecedently justified assumptions;
2. is not equally reproduced by a substantially weaker framework; and
3. either explains something the weaker framework cannot or yields a genuinely risky consequence.

## 9. Null-competitor discipline

The null competitor is not required to provide a deeper ontology. Its burden is to represent the predictive content of established GR + QFT + the Standard Model and their explicitly known limitations. A deeper framework must earn any claim of improvement.

## 10. Negative results are first-class

`NONFORCED` and `COUNTERMODELED` results are scientific outputs, not failures of the program. They must be preserved with the same provenance discipline as positive results.
