\section{Two Clifford structures, and why they must not be confused}
The source uses two related but different sets of operators. The first is a
system of real $16\times16$ matrices encoding the nine spatial Clifford
relations. The second is a family of 48 bounded operators acting on the
\emph{original complex fermionic Hilbert space}. Their indices and their
normalizations differ; merging them by an informal identification would
invalidate the later supercharge coefficients.

\begin{definition}[Spatial gamma matrices]
Let $\gamma_i\in\operatorname{Mat}_{16}(\mathbb R)$, $0\le i<9$,
be symmetric real matrices satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-clifford}
 \gamma_i\gamma_j+\gamma_j\gamma_i
 =2\delta_{ij}I_{16},\qquad \gamma_i^{\mathsf T}=\gamma_i.
\end{equation}
Define the antisymmetric two-index matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-two}
 \gamma^{ij}:=\frac12(\gamma_i\gamma_j-\gamma_j\gamma_i).
\end{equation}
These are source fields of \texttt{AlgebraData}, not arbitrary matrices
introduced to fit a later calculation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Original fermionic CAR]
For each spinor index $\alpha$ and color label $A$, the source supplies a
self-adjoint complex-linear operator
$\theta_{\alpha A}\in\mathcal L_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-car}
 \theta_{\alpha A}\theta_{\beta B}
 +\theta_{\beta B}\theta_{\alpha A}
 =\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{AB}I_{\mathcal F},
 \qquad \theta_{\alpha A}^{\dagger}=\theta_{\alpha A}.
\end{equation}
The source also requires that the common invariant complex submodules of
these operators are only $0$ and all of $\mathcal F$.
\end{definition}

One easy source of factor-of-two mistakes deserves emphasis. Taking
$\alpha=\beta$ and $A=B$ in \eqref{eq:theta-car} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-square}
 2\theta_{\alpha A}^2=I_{\mathcal F},\qquad
 \theta_{\alpha A}^2=\tfrac12 I_{\mathcal F}.
\end{equation}
Thus the source $\theta$'s are not the conventional generators whose
squares equal the identity. With $\Gamma_{\alpha A}=\sqrt2\,\theta_{\alpha A}$
one obtains the normalized relation
$\Gamma_p\Gamma_q+\Gamma_q\Gamma_p=2\delta_{pq}I$.
Any transfer from real Clifford elements into physical Fock operators must
preserve this normalization, including signs under the Spin double cover.

\section{Explicit mode pairing in the source normalization}
Order the 48 original labels into pairs $(p_a,q_a)$,
$a=1,\ldots,24$, and abbreviate the corresponding source
operators by $\theta_p$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-mode-pair}
 c_a=\frac{\theta_{p_a}+i\theta_{q_a}}{\sqrt{2}},
 \qquad c_a^\dagger=\frac{\theta_{p_a}-i\theta_{q_a}}{\sqrt{2}}.
\end{equation}
The factor $1/\sqrt2$ is forced by the original delta-one
Majorana anticommutator, not chosen from another Clifford convention.

\begin{proposition}[Complex mode CAR; conventional calculation]
The operators \eqref{eq:source-mode-pair} obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-complex-car}
 \{c_a,c_b\}=0,\qquad
 \{c_a^\dagger,c_b^\dagger\}=0,\qquad
 \{c_a,c_b^\dagger\}=\delta_{ab}I.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $n_a=c_a^\dagger c_a$ is a projection, and the
$n_a$ commute pairwise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed adjoint follows from source self-adjointness.
For a single pair, $\theta_{p_a}^2=\theta_{q_a}^2=I/2$
and $\{\theta_{p_a},\theta_{q_a}\}=0$, so
\[
 c_a^2=\frac12\bigl(\theta_{p_a}^2-\theta_{q_a}^2+
 i\{\theta_{p_a},\theta_{q_a}\}\bigr)=0.
\]
Similarly $c_ac_a^\dagger+c_a^\dagger c_a
=\theta_{p_a}^2+\theta_{q_a}^2=I$.
Distinct pairs anticommute because every cross-pair Majorana
anticommutator vanishes. Finally
$n_a^2=c_a^\dagger(1-n_a)c_a=n_a$ follows from $c_a^2=0$;
cross-pair CAR give $n_an_b=n_bn_a$.
\end{proof}
The standard occupation model $\bigwedge^\bullet\mathbb C^{24}$
has one basis vector per subset of the 24 modes and hence dimension
$2^{24}$. This calculation explains the dimension and normalization;
it does \emph{not} independently identify a new manuscript
representation with the accepted source algebra action. Nor is an
arbitrary pairing automatically Spin(9)- or SU(2)-equivariant:
implementers, center signs and gauge compatibility require distinct
source-level arguments.

\begin{sourceaccount}[Dimension count of the original Fock fiber]
At $N=2$ there are $16\cdot3=48$ real Majorana labels and 24 complex
fermion modes. The usual finite complex fermion Fock representation of
these modes has dimension $2^{24}$.
\end{sourceaccount}
\begin{proof}
Choose an ordering of the 48 real labels and pair consecutive normalized
Majorana generators into 24 complex annihilation--creation pairs. A
fermionic occupation configuration is a choice of occupation number
$n_a\in\{0,1\}$ for each mode $a=1,\dots,24$. There are precisely
$2^{24}$ such configurations. The source's \texttt{Fermion 2} is the complex
Euclidean space of that dimension. This counting argument does not by
itself identify an arbitrary abstract CAR module with the actual source
representation: that identification is the content of the separately
qualified original Clifford/Fock maps discussed next.
\end{proof}

\section{From an abstract Clifford algebra to the actual source operators}
Let $V_{48}$ be the real 48-label space and $\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathbb C}(V_{48})$
its complexified Clifford algebra with the source quadratic normalization.
The original CAR representation determines a complex algebra homomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clifford-fock-hom}
 \rho_{\mathrm{Cl}}:
 \mathrm{Cl}_{\mathbb C}(V_{48})\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F).
\end{equation}
At this level we are discussing the \emph{full finite-dimensional operator
algebra}; no global Spin(9) unitary representation on wavefunctions has yet
been invoked.

\begin{theorem}[Original Clifford--Fock algebra equivalence;
source-qualified]\label{thm:clifford-equivalence}
For the original 48-real-generator source and the original
$\mathcal F=\mathbb C^{2^{24}}$, the source complex Clifford action is
faithful and surjective. Hence it determines an isomorphism of complex
algebras
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clifford-end}
 \mathrm{Cl}_{48}^{\mathbb C}
 \;\cong\;\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Mathematical dependency argument]
The finite-dimensional source argument first establishes the image of the
original Clifford action as the full Fock endomorphism algebra (historical
M3D). A separate faithfulness theorem (M3H) proves that the same map is
injective; in the source, the matching finite ranks are used in the
injectivity reasoning. Surjectivity and injectivity give a bijective
complex-algebra homomorphism. M3I packages its inverse as an actual
\texttt{AlgEquiv} and proves its application law. Each step concerns the
same original complex Fock carrier, not an independent abstract model
named only by its dimension.

This paragraph is the conventional argument and dependency explanation,
not a claim that these few lines reproduce the full detailed Lean proof of
M3D or M3H. Those declarations and their exact consumer form the formal
proof evidence.
\end{proof}

\section{Why this equivalence does not solve the spatial group problem}
An algebra isomorphism is not yet a continuously chosen lift of a specific
$\operatorname{Spin}(9)$ action to unitary operators on $\mathcal F$. A
spatial transformation can induce an automorphism of
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F)$ without specifying a preferred
unitary implementer on $\mathcal F$; multiplying a unitary implementer by
an arbitrary scalar phase leaves its conjugation action unchanged. The
source Spin groups also possess a nontrivial central element, and its
sign matters when matching genuine spinor representations.

\begin{remark}[Two possible fibers]
The space $\mathcal F$ of physical fermion \emph{vectors} has complex
dimension $2^{24}$; the endomorphism algebra
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F)$ has dimension $2^{48}$.
A representation-theoretic bound established for one fiber must not be
reused for the other without proof. In particular, the conditional
first-weight thresholds 6 and 12 in the later spectral program belong to
different actions and should remain visibly distinct.
\end{remark}

\section{A methodological correction from the historical files}
The FCP source files and their integration copies have sometimes differed
by imports and local Lean instance syntax. The M3H historical source used
\texttt{letI}; an early integration port changed it to \texttt{let}. The
source-faithful port restored \texttt{letI}, after which a strict build
compiled the source and independent consumer but rejected the purely
stylistic \texttt{linter.style.haveILetI} suggestion. The subsequent
accepted repair retained the original theorem body and disabled only that
specific style suggestion; the final integrated strict run was green.
Thus a red workflow arising from a style warning must not be mistaken for
a false Clifford theorem. Nor should this experience license blanket
suppression of proof warnings or promotion of arbitrary changed ports.
