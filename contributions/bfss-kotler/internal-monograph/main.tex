\chapter*{Who this book is for}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Who this book is for}
This internal monograph is written for Evan Kotler, future BFSS project
leads, and research collaborators joining the mathematical work. Evan
should be able to use it to judge which routes are worth pursuing. An
incoming lead should be able to recover a theorem's exact scope and
dependencies without reconstructing the conversation that produced it.
A collaborator in operator theory, representation theory, or
mathematical physics should be able to follow the relevant argument
before opening the Lean source.

We assume familiarity with graduate linear algebra and basic Hilbert
space theory. The book introduces the BFSS carriers and conventions
as it needs them. Lean notation is explained where it affects a
statement, and gate identifiers are kept in the evidence notes whenever
possible. For onboarding, read Chapters~\ref{ch:scope} and
\ref{ch:carriers}, then the chapters on the physical Hamiltonian and
the massless interaction. For a research decision, start at
Chapter~\ref{ch:frontier}. For proof reuse, go from the mathematical
statement to its source declaration and receipt in
Appendix~\ref{app:atlas}.

\paragraph{Reading the evidence.}
A source account states what was checked for the specific source and
hypotheses. A conventional argument gives the mathematical reasoning
in the book, and conditional arguments display the premises still
owed. Numerical prescreens guide choices about what to test. Empirical
intake records the evidence a candidate must explain. The typeset
edition records an editorial synthesis. The exact source and original
verification receipts supply the evidence for formal qualifications.

\paragraph{Research cutoff.}
The unified integration source snapshot is
\texttt{fcd873ebbb31}, inspected on 23 November 2026 Pacific time.
The historical manuscript and theorem receipts retain their individual
tested commits. Appendix~\ref{app:atlas} preserves the Edition 0.8
inventory and provides a newer index with explicit scope and
supersessions. Consult the live repository checkpoint for decisions
after the cutoff.

\paragraph{Scientific purpose.}
The subject is the original relative SU(2) system. This revision follows
the massless multiplier into the native charge energy and through the
conditional zero-mode criteria. Chapter~\ref{ch:frontier} identifies
the estimates a physical trial sequence must still supply. The
spectral chapters explain the fidelity and positive point-spectrum
objectives. The topology and Standard Model chapters ask what would
allow an internal
mathematical construction to make contact with physics. They preserve
specific failed inferences and identify tests that could change the
research decision.

\emph{M Theory as a Matrix Model: A Conjecture},

\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.55.5112}{doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.55.5112}.

see the original mathematical literature including
H. Gluck, F. Warner and W. Ziller,
\emph{The geometry of the Hopf fibrations}, at
\url{https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/GluckWarnerZiller-HopfFibrations.pdf}.
All original-source Lean qualifications and research-only audits are
indexed in the companion evidence files; a literature citation is not
a proof that the cited physical interpretation holds for this fixed
relative SU(2) system.
