## What the fixed-ball criterion buys

On the original relative SU(2) physical space, let \(T\) be the minimal closed sixteen-charge column and \(H=T^\dagger T/16\). The qualified GM5E9 criterion uses a fixed smooth multiplier to recover a nonzero zero mode from a bounded sequence with vanishing charge and retained local norm. GM5E10 proposes deriving the needed bounds from probability in a closed ball and a supplied gauge-invariant IMS partition. The fixed-ball construction is in strict CI. The radial partition and the actual trial sequence remain separate mathematical obligations. [GM5E9 qualification](../integration/BFSS_GM5E9_R1_ORIGINAL_CUTOFF_ZERO_MODE_GREEN_ACCEPTANCE_2026_11_23.md) · [GM5E10 statement](../integration/BFSS_GM5E10_ORIGINAL_FIXED_BALL_ZERO_MODE_GATE_2026_11_23.md) · [Strict run](https://github.com/etblink/math/actions/runs/45231545024)

**A variational target with a fixed observable.** Write \(B_R=\mathbf1_{\{\|X\|\le R\}}\) and, for \(m>0\), consider the original invariant smooth-core problem
\[
    E_R(m)=\inf\left\{\|Tf\|^2/16 : \|f\|=1,\;\|B_R f\|^2\ge m\right\}.
\]
A certified \(E_R(m)=0\) for one pair \((R,m)\), with the partition in place, supplies the hypothesis of the fixed-ball criterion. Conversely a unit zero mode is approximable in the original charge graph by invariant smooth-core vectors. Normalize those vectors and use the contraction \(B_R\) to obtain convergence of their probability in that same ball. This gives \(E_R(m)=0\) for any \(m\) strictly below that mode's ball probability. Thus testing energy together with retained probability discriminates a threshold eigenvector from sequences that merely lower the energy by escaping. A finite residual in one trial vector supplies an upper bound on \(E_R(m)\). The required conclusion is a bound tending to zero at the same \(R\) and \(m\). ## A quantitative connection to the gravity comparison

The original BFSS conjecture concerns a limit of matrix quantum mechanics and **uncompactified eleven-dimensional M-theory**. In the light-front interpretation, a normalizable relative two-D0 zero mode supplies an internal threshold state in the \(N=2\) momentum sector. An actual identification requires our source Hamiltonian to be matched to the paper's closed operator and the decoupled center of mass restored. The existing light-front crosswalk already states the needed units and the operator-identification obligation. [Banks, Fischler, Shenker and Susskind](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.55.5112) · [Repository light-front source dictionary](../spectral-p2-original-field/P3_LIGHTFRONT_MASS_ENERGY_SOURCE_DICTIONARY_AND_2026_SU2_SPECTRAL_CROSSWALK_2026_11_13.md)

There is a more discriminating test than existence. Michel, Scheinpflug and Yin compare the calculated wavefunction's large-separation radial density with that inferred from the three-graviton amplitude. Bajaj and Lin independently extract the amplitude from a numerical wavefunction tail and report a separate four-point phase-shift calculation. These are same-observable theoretical comparisons with a specified normalization. The four-point calculation calls for independent scattering and continuum control. [Michel, Scheinpflug and Yin, §§IV.2–IV.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09837v1#S4) · [Bajaj and Lin, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14512v1) · [Herderschee and Maldacena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12592)

**New local-probability calculation.** Let \(\psi\) be a unit trial vector with certified fidelity \(p=\|P_{\ker H}\psi\|^2>0\), and \(u=P_{\ker H}\psi/\sqrt p\). Then
\[
d=\|u-\psi\|=\sqrt{2(1-\sqrt{p})}.
\]
For every measurable region with multiplication projection \(B\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \big(\max\{0,\|B\psi\|-d\}\big)^2
       \ \le\ \|Bu\|^2\ \le\ (\|B\psi\|+d)^2.
\]
Taking the preprint's reported \(p\ge0.9999978\) gives \(d\le0.001484\). Its trial-state second moment of the configuration norm is at most \(8.07420\) in its stated units. Markov's inequality at radius 6 gives trial-state ball probability at least \(1-8.07420/36\). The projection bound therefore gives **more than \(0.773\)** ball probability for its normalized projected state. This is a conventional deduction in that paper's setting. Applying the numbers to the repository requires the source and domain identification. The fidelity bound already implies kernel existence when transferred, so extracting an approximate core sequence from that kernel would be a cross-check between routes. [Michel, Scheinpflug and Yin, §IV.4 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09837v1#A2)

**The asymptotic test needs a finer error budget.** The tail variable \(r\) in the same paper is the largest singular value of \(X\). The ball criterion above uses the Frobenius norm \(\|X\|\). For \(p(r)\sim C r^{-10}\), \(C=1890\sqrt2\) in the paper's convention, a bounded annulus projection \(A_a=\mathbf1_{\{a\le r<2a\}}\) gives
\[
    \|A_a u\|^2\sim C(1-2^{-9})/(9a^9),\qquad
    \frac{\|A_{2a}u\|^2}{\|A_a u\|^2}\longrightarrow 2^{-9}.
\]
The leading-order annulus probability at \(a=10\) is about \(2.96\times10^{-7}\), or an amplitude of \(5.44\times10^{-4}\), below the global fidelity error in amplitude. This is a signal-budget estimate using the asymptotic formula. It warrants region-specific error and control of subleading terms before asserting a tail comparison there. An eventual reproduction should reserve annuli, publish the error on numerator and denominator, and include a basis or asymptotic matching procedure capable of representing a power tail. Finite Gaussian expansions can mimic it over a finite window. [Michel, Scheinpflug and Yin, §§IV.1–IV.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09837v1#S4)
