## Identity and weighted-form derivation

Let d=(a,i) be a color/spatial coordinate and t=sqrt(2) theta. With Q_alpha=-i/sqrt(2) sum_d T_{alpha,d} partial_d + B_alpha, T_{alpha,d}=sum_beta gamma_i[alpha,beta] t_{beta,a}, B_alpha=sum_{i<j,a,beta} (gamma_i gamma_j)[alpha,beta] (X_i cross X_j)_a t_{beta,a}. The latter uses f_abc=sqrt(2) epsilon_abc and original ordered-pair factor 1/2. A_de=sum_alpha T_d T_e, C_d=sum_alpha T_d B. Exact polynomial checks: 729 A_de gamma coefficient matches, 729 A_de+A_ed=32 delta_de I; sum_alpha B_alpha^2=16 V_B I (324 nonzero scalar monomials); sum_alpha B_alpha T_d=-C_d for all 27 coordinates; sum_d partial_d C_d=-16 L, L=sum X_ai gamma_i[alpha,beta] t_{alpha,b} t_{beta,c} cyclic, Y=i L/sqrt(2). R_e=sum_{d != e} X_d A_de matches B2(X,e) by the coefficient identity; D=sum_d X_d C_d matches CV(X)/i by independent triple gamma expansion.

For compactly supported smooth psi, or Gaussian-polynomial with justified decay, integration by parts (inner conjugate-linear first) and symmetry of second derivatives give the weighted form for W=1+||X||^2:
q_W = integral [ W (1/2 ||grad psi||^2 + <psi,(V_B+Y)psi>) - (1/16) sum_e <psi,R_e partial_e psi> - (sqrt(2)/16) <psi,CV psi> ].
Take real part or use polarized identity under integration. The kinetic off-diagonal integration by parts produces derivative of W=2X; the mixed term is the divergence of <psi,C_d psi> and produces Y plus radial CV. For W=1 both radial corrections vanish. B2 is expressed with t's, not theta's; archived jet normalization of beta/t includes a factor 1/2, so integrate104.py's -1/8 yields -1/16 on raw t contractions. CV is iD; its odd-degree scaling explains the extra sqrt(2). The sign of the integrated mixed term agrees with Y. This proves the algebraic reduction with the stated integration hypotheses; not verification that the 104 archived numerical integrals compute the literal source weighted form on its physical domain.
